What is lavender? Lavenders are small evergreen shrubs with gray-green hoary linear leaves. The purple flowers are sparsely arranged on spikes at the tips of long bare stalks and produce small nutlet fruits. The fragrance of the plant is caused by shining oil glands imbedded among tiny star-shaped trichomes (plant hairs) that cover the flowers, leaves, and stems. The plants in cultivation do not usually produce seed, and propagation is accomplished by cuttings or by dividing the roots.
Where does it come from? Lavender is a genus of 47 known species of perennial flowering plants in the mints family, Lamiaceae. It is native to the Old World, primarily found across the drier, warmer regions of mainland Eurasia, with an affinity for maritime breezes. Lavender is found on the Iberian Peninsula and around the entirety of the Mediterranean coastline including the Adriatic coast, the Balkans, the Levant, and coastal North Africa, in parts of Eastern and Southern Africa and the Middle East, as well as in South Asia and on the Indian subcontinent.
What are its benefits? Lavender has numerous medicinal and homeopathic uses as well. It has been used for centuries to treat various ailments, from headaches and stress to skin conditions like eczema and acne. Its antiseptic properties make it an effective treatment for cuts, burns, and insect bites, while its anti-inflammatory properties make it a natural remedy for arthritis and other joint conditions. Lavender oil is also a popular ingredient in many skin and hair care products due to its nourishing and rejuvenating properties. When applied to the skin, lavender oil can help to soothe and hydrate, leaving skin looking and feeling soft and smooth. In addition, lavender oil can help to promote hair growth and improve the health of your scalp, making it an ideal choice for those with dry, brittle, or thinning hair. Lavender has a calming effect on people who use it. Whether used in aromatherapy, as a bath additive, or simply grown in a garden, lavender has been shown to help reduce stress and anxiety, promote relaxation, and improve sleep. For those who struggle with insomnia or other sleep-related issues, incorporating lavender into their daily routine may help to promote restful, restorative sleep.
